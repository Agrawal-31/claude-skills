# Income-tax Act, 2025: what changes and how to handle it

## Status (checked October 2026)

- The Income-tax Act, 2025 received assent on 21 August 2025 and **came into force on
  1 April 2026**, replacing the Income-tax Act, 1961 (Income Tax Department press release,
  1 April 2026). The **Income-tax Rules, 2026** were notified on 20 March 2026 (G.S.R. 198(E))
  and also apply from 1 April 2026.
- **Income up to 31 March 2026** (FY 2025-26 and earlier) is still assessed under the 1961
  Act: returns are filed for "AY 2026-27" with 1961 section numbers (139(1), 139(4), 112A,
  234F...). Belated, revised and updated returns for those years also use the old Act.
- **Income from 1 April 2026** is "**tax year 2026-27**" under the 2025 Act. "Previous year"
  and "assessment year" are replaced by the single concept of tax year (s.3). The first return
  under the new Act is filed in 2027 (due 31 July 2027 for no business income, if unchanged).
- Budget 2026 left slabs and capital-gains rates unchanged (commentary); substance is mostly
  carried over, numbering is not.

## How to handle it in a conversation

1. Decide which Act governs from the **period the income was earned**, not the date of filing.
2. Use the numbering that the relevant ITR form uses; when explaining, give both
   ("s.112A of the 1961 Act, s.198 of the 2025 Act").
3. When a user quotes a section you don't recognise, it may be a 2025 Act number; check the
   mapping below or search "Income-tax Act 2025 section X corresponds to".
4. Notes and checklists written for one year must be re-mapped for the next; flag this in
   the post-filing record.
5. Re-verify forms and schedules for tax year 2026-27: the ITR forms are re-notified for
   the new Act and may differ from the AY 2026-27 layout described in this skill.

## Things that changed alongside (Finance Act 2026, per commentary; verify)

- Due date for non-audit business/professional income moved to 31 August (from AY 2026-27).
- Revised return window to 31 March after the year (12 months), fee for late revisions.
- Updated return possible after reassessment starts, with 10% extra additional tax.
- Buybacks taxed as capital gains for shareholders.
